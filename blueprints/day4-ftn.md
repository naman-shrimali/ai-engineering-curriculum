## ftn-01 — The Customization Decision

**Meta:** evolving/mixed · diff 3 · 120 min → 3,800–4,500 words (lean, decision-focused) · prereqs [api-02, rag-05] · related [ftn-02, ftn-03, api-06]. **The gatekeeper chapter — most readers should leave having decided NOT to fine-tune yet.**
**Thesis:** fine-tuning is the last customization lever to reach for, not the first — prompting, retrieval, and tools solve most problems cheaper and more reversibly; fine-tuning earns its cost only for behavior/format/latency goals that context can't buy, and never for knowledge (that's retrieval).
**Sections:** (1) Intuition: the customization ladder in cost/reversibility order — prompt (api-02) → few-shot → retrieval (rag-05) → tools (agt-02) → fine-tune → continued pretraining; climb only when the rung below provably plateaus (api-02's plateau tree, extended). (2) What fine-tuning is and isn't for: FOR — behavior/format/style/tone the prompt can't reliably hit, latency/cost (a small fine-tuned model replacing a big prompted one — ftn-06), tool-use reliability, domain *idiom*; NOT FOR — knowledge injection (the cardinal error — fnd-07's SFT-doesn't-add-knowledge, retrieval does; fine-tuning teaches the *format* of knowing and amplifies hallucination on ungrounded facts). (3) The decision framework: the diagnostic questions (is it a knowledge gap → RAG; a behavior gap prompting can't close → maybe FT; a cost/latency gap → distillation, ftn-06) and the cost/benefit honest accounting (data curation dominates — ftn-03; the ownership tax: you now own a model version, its evals, its drift, its retraining). (4) The prerequisites gate: you cannot fine-tune well without an eval (evl-01 — how would you know it worked?) and clean data (ftn-03) — teams that skip these produce confidently-worse models. (5) When it clearly pays: worked scenarios (structured-output reliability at scale, a consistent persona, a latency-critical narrow task) vs when it clearly doesn't (the "fine-tune on our docs" anti-pattern, retold from fnd-07).
**Must land:** fine-tuning is last, not first; knowledge → retrieval, behavior → maybe fine-tune (the load-bearing distinction); data curation is the real cost; no eval means no fine-tune; the ownership tax is forever.
**Diagrams:** (1) *graph TD* the customization decision tree (knowledge/behavior/cost/latency gap → lever); (2) cost/reversibility ladder table.
**Mini-project:** for three real capstone needs, run the decision framework to a documented verdict (expect ≥2 to land on prompt/RAG, not FT); for the one that might warrant FT, write the go/no-go with its data and eval prerequisites. Success: a customization decision log — including justified "don't fine-tune" outcomes. Capstone: the capstone's customization strategy, evidence-based.
**Sources:** [T1] provider fine-tuning guides (OpenAI/together — when-to-use sections) · [T2] reuse Ouyang InstructGPT arXiv:2203.02155 · [T4] a lab/practitioner "RAG vs fine-tuning" engineering post · [T2] reuse fnd-06 scaling/data lessons.
**Volatile fences:** provider FT offerings; the ladder and the knowledge-vs-behavior distinction are permanent.

## ftn-02 — Fine-Tuning Methods

**Meta:** evolving/mixed · diff 4 · 300 min → 6,200–7,000 words · prereqs [fnd-05, ftn-01] · related [ftn-04, ftn-05, prd-03]. **The module's technical flagship.**
**Thesis:** the method spectrum — full fine-tuning, LoRA/QLoRA, and PEFT variants — is a memory/quality/portability trade governed by the fnd-02 training-memory math; LoRA's insight (adaptation is low-rank) collapses the footprint enough to fine-tune large models on modest hardware, which is why it's the practitioner default.
**Sections:** (1) Intuition: full fine-tuning updates every weight (fnd-02's loop at scale — and fnd-02's ~16 bytes/param training footprint says why that's expensive: a 7B model needs ~112GB); the question is how much you can freeze. (2) Full fine-tuning: mechanics (the fnd-02 loop, all params, small LR, few epochs — overfitting is back, fnd-02), memory reality (optimizer states dominate — the ZeRO/offload landscape in one paragraph), when it's justified (rarely for app engineers — large behavior shifts, big data). (3) LoRA at depth (the chapter's core): the low-rank insight (weight *updates* during adaptation are approximately low-rank, so learn A·B instead of ΔW — cite Hu et al.); the math that earns its place (rank r, ΔW = BA with B∈d×r, A∈r×d; params drop from d² to 2dr; the r and alpha knobs and what they trade); why quality ≈ full FT for most adaptation; adapters as portable/swappable/composable artifacts. (4) QLoRA: quantize the frozen base to 4-bit, train adapters in higher precision (cite Dettmers) — the democratizer (65B on one GPU); the quality/memory trade (near-lossless in practice, api-07's quantization lessons); the memory napkin (base-quantized + tiny adapter + optimizer-on-adapter-only). (5) Choosing: the method decision (data size × hardware × portability needs × quality bar → full/LoRA/QLoRA), serving implications (adapter hot-swapping vs merged weights — prd-02/03 tie-in), and the eval-per-method discipline (api-06 — every method+config is a model version).
**Must land:** the fnd-02 memory math is why methods exist (make the reader recompute it); LoRA = adaptation is low-rank (the one insight); QLoRA democratized it (quantize base, train adapter); adapters are portable artifacts; LoRA is the default, full FT the exception, for app engineers.
**Math that earns its place:** LoRA parameter count (d² → 2dr); training-memory comparison across methods (extends fnd-02's). The chapter's signature.
**Diagrams:** (1) *graph TD* the method decision tree; (2) *graph LR* LoRA: frozen W + trainable low-rank B·A → merged or swapped adapter (the mechanism).
**Mini-project:** LoRA-fine-tune a small open model (api-07 stack) on a tiny behavior task (e.g. a consistent output format your prompt struggles with); compute the memory footprint three ways (full/LoRA/QLoRA) before running; verify the smoke test (overfit 50 examples — fnd-02) before the real run. Success: a working adapter + the memory math confirmed on your hardware. Capstone: an adapter for the capstone's one FT-justified need (from ftn-01).
**Sources:** [T2] Hu et al., LoRA, arXiv:2106.09685 · [T2] Dettmers et al., QLoRA, arXiv:2305.14314 · [T2] Rajbhandari ZeRO arXiv:1910.02054 (reuse fnd-02) · [T1] HuggingFace PEFT docs · [T1] provider fine-tuning API docs · reuse Dettmers LLM.int8 arXiv:2208.07339.
**Volatile fences:** PEFT-variant zoo (light); LoRA/QLoRA core and the memory math are stable.

## ftn-03 — Data for Fine-Tuning

**Meta:** evolving/mixed · diff 4 · 180 min → 4,300–5,000 words · prereqs [ftn-01, evl-02] · related [ftn-04, ftn-05, evl-02]. **The chapter that decides success — data quality dominates method choice.**
**Thesis:** fine-tuning outcomes are decided by data quality far more than method or hyperparameters (fnd-02/fnd-06's lesson at fine-tune scale) — curation, formatting, and decontamination are the real work, and a hundred excellent examples beat ten thousand mediocre ones.
**Sections:** (1) Intuition: the spec is the dataset (fnd-02), sharpened — a fine-tune learns exactly what the data demonstrates, including its errors, biases, and inconsistencies; garbage in, confidently-fluent garbage out. (2) Quality over quantity (the core evidence): the LIMA result (a thousand curated examples rival massive SFT sets — cite Zhou) and why (pretraining already installed capability, fnd-06; SFT elicits format — fnd-07; so consistency and quality of demonstration dominate volume); the "clean 100 beats dirty 10k" doctrine. (3) Curation mechanics: sourcing (production traces via the evl-04 flywheel — the highest-value source; expert authoring; synthetic with verification — evl-02's methods, training edition), the consistency imperative (contradictory examples teach contradiction — worse than fewer examples), diversity coverage (the input distribution the model must handle), and difficulty/edge-case inclusion. (4) Formatting: the chat-template discipline (fnd-07/api-07 — wrong template silently ruins the run; loss-masking to completion tokens — fnd-07's gotcha, made concrete), input/output pair construction, and preference-pair construction preview (ftn-05). (5) The hygiene gates (evl-02 transferred): decontamination against the eval set (the cardinal sin — train/test leakage, fnd-02; a fine-tune that memorizes eval cases reports fake success), dedup, PII scrubbing (sec-03 — training data is a liability surface, fnd-02/fnd-06 memorization), and the labeling-quality discipline (evl-02's agreement methods).
**Must land:** data quality dominates method (the module's most important sentence); consistency beats volume (contradictions are poison); production traces are the best source (the flywheel again); decontaminate against evals (leakage = fake success); wrong chat template silently kills the run.
**Diagrams:** (1) *graph LR* the data pipeline: source → curate/dedup → format+template → decontaminate → split → train (mirrors fnd-06's ingestion, fine-tune scale); (2) quality-vs-quantity evidence table.
**Mini-project:** build the dataset for your ftn-02 adapter properly: 100+ examples curated for consistency, formatted with the correct template (verify at token level — api-07), decontaminated against your eval, split held-out; then deliberately inject 20 contradictory examples, retrain, and measure the quality drop. Success: a clean dataset + a demonstrated contradiction-poisoning effect. Capstone: the capstone adapter's training data, done right.
**Sources:** [T2] Zhou et al., LIMA, arXiv:2305.11206 · [T2] Liu et al., "What Makes Good Data for Alignment" arXiv:2312.15685 (reuse evl-02) · [T2] reuse Carlini extraction arXiv:2012.07805 (PII) · [T1] provider fine-tuning data-format docs · [T4] a data-curation engineering post (reuse fnd-06's FineWeb as the pretraining-scale analogue).
**Volatile fences:** minimal; data doctrine is evergreen-adjacent.

## ftn-04 — Fine-Tuning in Practice

**Meta:** evolving/volatile · diff 4 · 240 min → 5,000–6,200 words · prereqs [ftn-02, ftn-03] · related [ftn-05, prd-03, evl-06]
**Thesis:** running a fine-tune to a shippable result is an empirical loop — hyperparameters, monitoring the training/validation curves (fnd-02), evaluating the result against the base, and deciding go/no-go — executable via provider APIs (managed) or open-source stacks (controlled), with the same eval discipline as everything else in the repo.
**Sections:** (1) Intuition: fine-tuning is an experiment (fnd-02's experiment discipline — one change at a time, logged) with a slow expensive inner loop, so you de-risk before spending (smoke test, tiny run, then scale). (2) The two paths: managed provider fine-tuning (upload data, get a model — convenient, limited control, provider-hosted serving) vs open-source stacks (Axolotl/TRL/Unsloth-class — full control, you own serving via api-07/prd-02); the choose-by-need decision (api-06 logic). (3) Hyperparameters that matter (practitioner subset): learning rate (fnd-02 — the sensitive one), epochs (overfitting watch — fnd-02's curves, small data means few epochs), LoRA rank/alpha (ftn-02), batch size; the "start from provider/community defaults, tune by eval" doctrine (don't grid-search blindly). (4) Monitoring and diagnosis: reading training vs validation loss curves (fnd-02's overfitting signature — the canonical skill, now on your own run), the smoke test (overfit 50 examples — fnd-02), loss-not-decreasing / diverging / overfitting playbook, catastrophic forgetting (the fine-tune erodes base capabilities — evaluate on general tasks too) and safety-erosion (fnd-07/sec-05 — SFT can strip alignment; re-run safety evals — sec-04). (5) Evaluation and go/no-go: the fine-tuned-vs-base bake-off (api-06/eng-11 — did it actually beat the prompt-only baseline, the honest bar many fine-tunes fail), regression on general capability + safety, and the ship decision with its serving/ownership plan.
**Must land:** de-risk before spending (smoke → tiny → scale); read the loss curves (fnd-02's skill, live); the honest bar is beating the prompt-only baseline (many fine-tunes lose); check catastrophic forgetting AND safety erosion; managed vs OSS is a control/ownership trade.
**Diagrams:** (1) *graph LR* the fine-tuning workflow: data → smoke test → tiny run → full run → eval vs base → go/no-go; (2) annotated training/validation loss curve (the fnd-02 overfitting figure, fine-tune edition — describe for text or tiny ```text``` sketch).
**Mini-project:** run your ftn-02/ftn-03 fine-tune to completion: smoke test, monitor the curves, evaluate against the prompt-only baseline AND on a general-capability probe (forgetting check) AND the safety subset (sec-04); make a documented go/no-go. Success: a fine-tuned model with an honest bake-off verdict (including a valid "the prompt baseline won" outcome). Capstone: the capstone's adapter, shipped or rejected with evidence.
**Sources:** [T1] provider fine-tuning API docs (OpenAI/Anthropic/together) · [T1] HuggingFace TRL + PEFT docs · [T1] Axolotl docs (OSS exemplar) · [T2] reuse LoRA/QLoRA arXiv:2106.09685 / 2305.14314 · [T4] a fine-tuning-in-practice engineering post (select current).
**Volatile fences:** tooling and provider offerings (heavy — fence the stacks); the experiment discipline, curve-reading, and go/no-go bar are stable.

## ftn-05 — Preference Optimization & RL

**Meta:** evolving/mixed · diff 5 · 240 min → 5,000–6,200 words · prereqs [fnd-07, ftn-02] · related [ftn-04, sec-05, agt-03]
**Thesis:** when SFT's imitation ceiling isn't enough, preference optimization (DPO and kin) and RL from verifiable rewards push models beyond demonstration quality — the fnd-07 pipeline, now hands-on: DPO for accessible offline preference tuning, verifiable-reward RL for reasoning, both governed by data quality and reward-design honesty.
**Sections:** (1) Intuition: SFT imitates demonstrations (ceiling = demonstrator); preferences teach *relative* quality (judging beats producing — fnd-07), so preference optimization exceeds the SFT ceiling; the fnd-07 pipeline (SFT → preferences → verifiable rewards) as the roadmap, now buildable. (2) DPO at depth (the accessible workhorse — fnd-07 recap → practice): the insight (the policy implicitly defines a reward, so optimize preferences directly, no reward model, no RL loop — cite Rafailov); the loss intuition (raise logprob of chosen, lower rejected, KL-anchored to reference — the fnd-07 KL leash, now a hyperparameter β); preference-pair data (ftn-03's construction, the hard part — where pairs come from, consistency); when DPO suffices (most app-level preference tuning). (3) The DPO-variant landscape honestly (fnd-07's fence): KTO, ORPO, IPO exist and trade specifics — one paragraph, pointer, don't overinvest (volatile). (4) RLHF and online RL (when offline isn't enough — fnd-07): the reward-model + PPO path (heavier, exploration, rarely app-engineer territory — teach for literacy), and why it persists (online exploration DPO can't do). (5) Verifiable-reward RL (the reasoning frontier — fnd-07's DeepSeek-R1 story, hands-on-adjacent): where an automatic verifier exists (code/math/format), RL can push past preference ceilings (GRPO-class); the reward-design honesty (reward hacking — fnd-07/sec-05 — the reward IS the spec, and a gameable reward gets gamed); the accessibility reality (compute-heavy — most engineers consume reasoning models via API, agt-03, rather than train them; teach the mechanism, set the expectation).
**Must land:** preferences exceed the SFT imitation ceiling (why they exist); DPO democratized it (no reward model/RL loop — the accessible default); β is the KL leash (fnd-07 made concrete); verifiable rewards power reasoning but need honest reward design (reward = spec = gameable); most engineers consume RL'd models, not train them (set expectations).
**Math that earns its place:** the DPO objective at intuition level (chosen vs rejected logprobs, β·KL anchor); reward-hacking as Goodhart (reuse fnd-07's objective).
**Diagrams:** (1) *graph LR* the fnd-07 pipeline hands-on: SFT → DPO (offline pairs) → verifiable-reward RL, with data sources per stage; (2) DPO vs RLHF trade table.
**Mini-project:** DPO-tune your ftn-04 model on a small preference set (construct pairs from the ftn-03 discipline — chosen/rejected on a quality dimension your eval measures); sweep β and observe the quality/divergence trade (the KL leash, felt); evaluate against the SFT-only version. Success: a DPO'd model beating its SFT baseline on the target dimension, β trade observed. Capstone: optional preference-tuning of the capstone adapter; most will stop at SFT (valid).
**Sources:** [T2] Rafailov et al., DPO, arXiv:2305.18290 · [T2] Ouyang InstructGPT arXiv:2203.02155 (reuse) · [T2] DeepSeek-R1 arXiv:2501.12948 (reuse, GRPO/verifiable) · [T2] Ethayarajh KTO arXiv:2402.01306 · [T1] HuggingFace TRL (DPO/GRPO) docs · [T4] Lambert RLHF Book (reuse fnd-07, T4).
**Volatile fences:** the preference-method zoo and RL-recipe frontier (heavy — fnd-07's fence, deepened); DPO core, the KL-leash concept, and reward-hacking honesty are stable.

## ftn-06 — Distillation & Small Language Models

**Meta:** evolving/mixed · diff 4 · 180 min → 4,300–5,000 words · prereqs [ftn-02, api-06] · related [ftn-04, prd-03, fro-03, api-07]
**Thesis:** distillation compresses a capable teacher's behavior into a smaller, cheaper, faster student — the highest-ROI structural cost lever (eng-10) for stable high-volume narrow tasks, and the bridge to on-device (fro-03); the distill-vs-buy calculation decides when to run it.
**Sections:** (1) Intuition: you don't need frontier capability for a narrow task, you need frontier capability *distilled into* a small model for that task — the overtraining-economics insight (fnd-06) applied deliberately by you. (2) The distillation methods: response/data distillation (the practical default — teacher generates training data, student SFTs on it; this is fine-tuning, ftn-02/03/04, with a model as the labeler — the accessible path), logit/soft-label distillation (student matches teacher distributions — richer signal, needs teacher access), and the reasoning-trace distillation angle (distill a reasoning model's traces into a cheaper student — fnd-07/agt-03; a current hot path, fence). (3) The distill-vs-buy calculation (api-06/eng-10 economics): when distillation pays (stable narrow task + high sustained volume + latency/cost/privacy pressure — the ftn-06 sweet spot) vs when the API wins (broad/changing tasks, low volume — the ownership tax isn't worth it); the honest break-even. (4) Small language models as targets: the capability floor of SLMs (fnd-09's bands — what small models reliably do; the api-07 laptop-tier reality), the quality gap accounting (measure on your eval — the distilled student must pass the same bar), and the serving payoff (prd-02/03 — small = cheap = fast = deployable-on-device, fro-03 bridge). (5) The workflow: teacher selection → task-data generation (with verification — the teacher hallucinates too, fnd-09) → student fine-tune (ftn-04) → the bake-off (student vs teacher-via-API on cost/latency/quality — eng-11) → deploy decision; the licensing note (some model terms restrict distillation — sec-03/api-06, check).
**Must land:** distillation = fine-tune with a model as labeler (the accessible framing); it's eng-10's top structural lever for stable narrow high-volume tasks; the distill-vs-buy calc decides (ownership tax vs unit economics); the student passes the same eval bar; check distillation licensing.
**Diagrams:** (1) *graph LR* the distillation workflow: teacher → generate+verify data → student fine-tune → bake-off → deploy; (2) *graph TD* the distill-vs-buy decision.
**Mini-project:** distill a capstone sub-task: pick a narrow high-volume task your capstone does with a big model, generate+verify training data from it, fine-tune a small model (ftn-04 stack), and bake off student-vs-teacher on quality/cost/latency (eng-11). Success: a small model passing the eval bar at a fraction of cost — or an honest verdict that the gap is too wide. Capstone: a distilled component for the capstone's highest-volume route; bridges to fro-03.
**Sources:** [T2] Hinton et al., distillation, arXiv:1503.02531 (the origin) · [T2] a sequence-level KD / on-policy distillation paper (e.g. Agarwal GKD arXiv:2306.13649) · [T2] reuse fnd-06 overtraining economics + Touvron LLaMA arXiv:2302.13971 · [T1] provider distillation/fine-tuning docs · [T4] a distillation-in-production engineering post.
**Volatile fences:** SLM landscape and reasoning-distillation frontier; the distill-vs-buy logic and "fine-tune with a labeler" framing are stable.
