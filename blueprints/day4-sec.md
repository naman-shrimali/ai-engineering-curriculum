## sec-01 — Prompt Injection & the LLM Threat Model

**Meta:** evolving/mixed · diff 3 · 240 min → 5,000–6,200 words · prereqs [agt-01, rag-05] · related [sec-02, sec-04, eng-02, eng-09]. **The module's foundation — every other sec chapter and eng-09 builds on it.**
**Thesis:** the one fact that reorganizes LLM security — instructions and data share a single channel with no reliable in-band separator — makes prompt injection unsolvable at the prompt layer and forces every real defense outward into privileges, validation, and isolation.
**Sections:** (1) Intuition: the classic security boundary (code vs data) doesn't exist inside a context window — everything is tokens the model may treat as instructions (fnd-04/fnd-05); this is architectural, not a bug awaiting a patch. (2) The threat taxonomy (teach eng-09's table): direct injection (user overrides system prompt, jailbreaks, prompt extraction — why the system prompt is neither secret nor binding, api-01), indirect injection (the dangerous one — instructions in retrieved docs/tool results/web pages/images; cite Greshake; the RAG and agent surfaces from eng-01/eng-02), the OWASP LLM Top 10 as the field's shared map. (3) Why in-band defenses are incomplete (the load-bearing argument): delimiters, "treat as data" framing, and instruction-hierarchy training *lower* success rates but can't guarantee — attacker adapts, and the channel is shared; measured attack-success-rate reductions, not zeros (cite instruction-hierarchy work honestly). (4) The exfiltration completion (why injection matters): injected instructions need an output channel — rendered links, tool calls, markdown images encoding secrets in URLs; the attack is injection + exfiltration, so blocking either link helps. (5) The defense posture (points to eng-09's rules, teaches the why): authorization outside the model (ACL-at-retrieval, session-derived tool privilege — eng-02), least privilege bounds blast radius (the union-of-tool-privileges argument), human gates on consequential actions, output handling (sanitize before render, generated-URLs-as-exfiltration); contain, don't just filter (sec-02 forward).
**Must land:** the shared-channel fact is the whole chapter — everything follows from it; in-band defenses are rate-reductions not guarantees (design as if injection succeeds); blast radius = tool privileges; injection+exfiltration is the real attack; defenses live outside the prompt.
**Diagrams:** (1) *graph TD* the trust-boundary reality: every context source labeled trusted/untrusted, flowing into one window; (2) *sequenceDiagram* an indirect-injection-to-exfiltration attack through a RAG-fed agent (the canonical scenario).
**Mini-project:** attack your own capstone (rag-05 + agt-01): plant an injected instruction in a retrieved document, attempt prompt extraction and a tool-misuse via the content, and an exfiltration via a generated link; then verify which eng-09 defenses (least privilege, human gate, output sanitization) actually contain each. Success: a self-injection you landed and a defense that contained it. Capstone: the capstone's threat model documented; feeds sec-04.
**Sources:** [T2] OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications · [T2] Greshake et al., indirect injection, arXiv:2302.12173 · [T2] Wallace et al., instruction hierarchy, arXiv:2404.13208 · [T2] Perez & Ribeiro, prompt injection/extraction, arXiv:2211.09527 · [T1] provider prompt-injection/safety guidance.
**Volatile fences:** specific jailbreak techniques and defense-tool efficacy; the shared-channel fact and outward-defense doctrine are permanent.

## sec-02 — Guardrails & Content Moderation

**Meta:** evolving/mixed · diff 3 · 180 min → 4,300–5,000 words · prereqs [sec-01] · related [sec-04, eng-09, evl-03]
**Thesis:** guardrails are the input/output filtering layer that *reduces* rates and *catches* known-bad — a necessary complement to sec-01's structural defenses, never a substitute; the engineering is layering (structural + model-based + policy) with honest accounting of false-positive/negative trade-offs.
**Sections:** (1) Intuition: sec-01 said containment beats filtering — guardrails are the filtering that still earns its place (defense in depth: cheap, fast, catches the obvious, buys time), sitting as eng-04's safety wrapper around the gateway. (2) The layered stack: structural (schema/enum constraints as guardrails — api-03; the cheapest, strongest where applicable), input filters (injection classifiers, PII detection, topic/policy filters — pre-model), output filters (moderation, PII leak detection, groundedness gates — post-model, pre-render), and policy models (a model judging against a written policy — RLAIF's cousin, fnd-07; evl-03's judge machinery pointed at safety). (3) Content moderation specifically: provider moderation endpoints (T1) vs self-hosted classifiers vs LLM-judged policy; the harm taxonomy (what you're actually filtering); multimodal moderation (api-04 — pixels too). (4) The trade-off accounting (the honest core): every filter has FP/FN rates; over-blocking is a product-killing failure mode (the over-refusal cousin — fnd-07), under-blocking is a safety failure; tuning thresholds on a labeled adversarial set (sec-04's suite; evl-01 discipline — the eval decides guardrails too). (5) Where guardrails belong in the system: gateway wrapper (eng-04), the retry/route-around on soft blocks, logging blocked events (the sec-04 red-team feedstock + the abuse-signal), and the "filter is not containment" reminder (sec-01 — a 95% filter on an unbounded agent is theater, eng-09 rule 7).
**Must land:** guardrails complement, never replace, structural defenses (sec-01); layer them (structural → input → output → policy); FP/FN is a measured product trade-off, not a solved problem; over-blocking kills products; filters feed the red-team loop.
**Diagrams:** (1) *graph LR* the layered guardrail pipeline wrapping a model call (input filters → model → output filters → render), with block/route-around paths; (2) FP/FN trade-off table by layer.
**Mini-project:** add a guardrail layer to the capstone gateway: input PII detection + an output moderation check + a groundedness gate (reuse evl-03's judge); build a 30-case set (15 should-pass, 15 should-block including 5 borderline), tune thresholds, measure FP/FN. Success: a measured FP/FN operating point you chose deliberately. Capstone: the capstone's safety wrapper.
**Sources:** [T1] OpenAI moderation API docs · [T2] Llama Guard, arXiv:2312.06674 · [T2] Inan et al. / NeMo Guardrails, arXiv:2310.10501 · [T1] provider safety-best-practices docs · reuse Wallace instruction-hierarchy arXiv:2404.13208.
**Volatile fences:** moderation-tool landscape and model efficacy; the layering doctrine and FP/FN accounting are stable.

## sec-03 — Privacy, Data Governance & Compliance

**Meta:** evolving/mixed · diff 2 · 180 min → 4,300–5,000 words · prereqs [api-01] · related [sec-01, eng-09, ftn-03]
**Thesis:** LLM systems create novel data-governance surfaces — prompts and traces holding PII, provider data-use terms, embedding inversion, training-data memorization, deletion propagation — and the engineer's job is mapping data flows, applying purpose limitation and minimization, and treating regulatory context (GDPR, EU AI Act) as design input, not legal afterthought.
**Sections:** (1) Intuition: every LLM call potentially ships user data to a third party and logs it in three places (trace store, provider, any eval harvest) — governance is knowing and controlling those flows (the data-flow map from eng-09's checklist). (2) The novel surfaces: prompts/traces as PII stores (redaction before storage — eng-08's warning; the retrofit-is-an-incident lesson), provider data-use terms (training-on-inputs, retention, residency — the api-06 selection criterion made concrete), embedding inversion (vectors reconstruct text — fnd-03; vector stores inherit source ACLs), training-data memorization (fnd-06's extraction result — the fine-tuning-on-user-data risk, ftn-03 forward), and the RAG deletion problem (right-to-be-forgotten means propagating deletes through index + cache + traces — rag-05). (3) The principles applied: data minimization (don't send/log what you don't need — also a cost and blast-radius win), purpose limitation (traces harvested for evals vs used for training — different consents), retention policies, PII detection/redaction pipelines (sec-02's tooling, governance framing). (4) The regulatory map for engineers (not lawyers): GDPR touchpoints (lawful basis, DSARs, deletion, DPAs with providers), the EU AI Act risk tiers at orientation level (what "high-risk" obligations imply for logging/human-oversight/documentation), sector regimes (HIPAA/financial as governance-decides-self-hosting — api-07's criterion 1); the honest "consult counsel, but design so counsel can say yes" stance. (5) The self-hosting governance win (api-07 recap): data never leaves — when the flow map has a hard boundary, self-hosting is the architecture, not a preference.
**Must land:** map the data flows first (can't govern unknown flows); redaction before first storage (retrofit = incident); embeddings are data (inversion); deletion must propagate everywhere (RAG's hard compliance problem); regulation is design input; governance constraints can decide self-hosting outright.
**Diagrams:** (1) *graph LR* the data-flow map: user → prompt → provider + trace store + eval harvest + index, with governance controls (redaction, retention, ACL, consent) at each edge.
**Mini-project:** produce the capstone's data-flow map and governance controls: classify every data sink, add trace redaction, document provider data-use posture, and implement deletion propagation (delete a doc → gone from index + cache + traces). Success: a data-flow map a DPO could review + working deletion propagation. Capstone: the capstone's governance documentation.
**Sources:** [T1] provider data-usage/privacy & DPA docs (OpenAI/Anthropic enterprise terms) · [T2] Carlini et al., training-data extraction, arXiv:2012.07805 (reuse fnd-06) · [T2] embedding-inversion paper, arXiv:2310.06816 · [T1] EU AI Act official summary / GDPR text (official sources only) · [T1] provider compliance/certifications pages.
**Volatile fences:** specific regulatory timelines and provider terms; the flow-mapping method and principles are stable. Add a "not legal advice" note in the lede.

## sec-04 — Red-Teaming LLM Applications

**Meta:** evolving/mixed · diff 4 · 180 min → 4,300–5,000 words · prereqs [sec-01, evl-02] · related [sec-02, eng-09, evl-06]
**Thesis:** you find your system's failures before adversaries do by building an adversarial eval suite — red-teaming is evl-02 pointed at harm, run as a CI gate (evl-06), turning security from a one-time audit into a regression-protected property.
**Sections:** (1) Intuition: red-teaming is evaluation with an adversary's objective — the evl-01 harness (eng-03) loaded with attacks instead of tasks; the shift from "does it work" to "how does it break." (2) The attack surface catalogue (operationalizes sec-01): direct injection/jailbreak attempts, indirect injection via each content source (RAG docs, tool results, images — api-04), prompt extraction, exfiltration attempts, tool-misuse/excessive-agency (eng-02's blast radius), and harm-category probes (sec-02's taxonomy). (3) Building the adversarial suite (evl-02 method, adversarial data): sourcing attacks (known jailbreak corpora, published techniques, your own domain-specific vectors), automated red-teaming (LLM-generates-attacks — cite Perez; scalable coverage), and the key discipline — every real incident and every published new technique becomes a suite case (the flywheel, security edition). (4) Running it as a gate (evl-06 integration): red-team cases as *hard* CI gates (eng-09's checklist line), attack-success-rate as a tracked metric (trending toward zero, never at zero — sec-01's honesty), regression protection (a fixed jailbreak stays fixed). (5) The human element: scoped manual red-teaming for creative attacks automation misses, bug-bounty-style programs, and the responsible-disclosure posture; the honest scope note (product security ≠ frontier model safety — that's the labs' red-teaming, sec-05 adjacent).
**Must land:** red-teaming = adversarial evals in CI (not a one-time pentest); every attack surface from sec-01 gets cases; attack-success-rate trends down but never zeroes (design accordingly); automated + manual; new techniques become regression cases forever.
**Diagrams:** (1) *graph TD* the attack-surface catalogue → suite categories → CI gate; (2) *graph LR* the red-team flywheel (incident/publication → case → gate → regression protection).
**Mini-project:** build a 40-case adversarial suite for the capstone (from sec-01's self-attacks + a public jailbreak set + 5 domain-specific), score attack-success-rate, wire it as a hard gate into the evl-06 CI, then fix one landed attack and watch the gate hold. Success: a security regression suite gating your capstone. Capstone: the capstone's red-team suite; the security half of its portfolio story (eng-12).
**Sources:** [T2] Perez et al., red-teaming LMs with LMs, arXiv:2202.03286 · [T2] Zou et al., universal adversarial attacks, arXiv:2307.15043 · [T2] Chao et al., PAIR jailbreaks, arXiv:2310.08419 · [T1] OWASP LLM Top 10 (reuse sec-01) · [T4] a provider/lab red-teaming methodology post.
**Volatile fences:** specific attack techniques (churn constantly); the adversarial-evals-as-CI method is permanent.

## sec-05 — Alignment & Safety for Engineers

**Meta:** stable/evergreen · diff 3 · 180 min → 4,300–5,000 words · prereqs [fnd-07] · related [sec-01, sec-04, fnd-09]
**Thesis:** alignment concepts that were abstract in fnd-07 have concrete product consequences — specification gaming, reward hacking, misuse-vs-misalignment, and the limits of RLHF shape how engineers design objectives, evals, and guardrails; you don't need to solve alignment, but you must recognize its footprints in your own systems.
**Sections:** (1) Intuition: alignment is "the system optimizes what you asked, which isn't what you meant" (fnd-02's spec-is-the-dataset, fnd-07's reward-model imperfection) — a problem engineers meet daily at product scale, not just labs meet at frontier scale. (2) The concepts with their engineering footprints: specification gaming / reward hacking (fnd-07 — your LLM-judge gets gamed, your metric Goodharts, evl-01/evl-03; the eng-05 verifier's own reward-hacking risk), sycophancy (fnd-07 — the product consequence: never validate with model agreement), the limits of RLHF (helpful/harmless/honest can conflict; over-refusal as mis-generalization — sec-02's FP problem is an alignment artifact). (3) Misuse vs misalignment (the clarifying distinction): misuse = the model does what a bad actor asks (sec-01/sec-02/sec-04's domain — a product-security problem); misalignment = the model does what no one asked (specification/goal problems — mostly labs', but agent objective-design touches it, agt-03/agt-09); why conflating them muddles defenses. (4) What engineers own vs what labs own: you own your system's objective design (evals, reward signals in any fine-tuning — ftn-05), your guardrails, your agent authorization; labs own model-level alignment; the interface (model cards, safety training you inherit and can erode via fine-tuning — ftn-03's warning). (5) Responsible-AI practice for engineers: honest capability communication (fnd-09 — the jagged frontier as an ethics issue: don't oversell), the human-oversight design for consequential decisions (sec-03's regulatory tie-in, eng-02's gates as an alignment control), and the disposition — engineering humility about a system you can't fully specify.
**Must land:** alignment footprints appear in your evals and agents (not just at labs); reward hacking targets your metrics (evl-03's judge is a reward model); misuse ≠ misalignment (different defenses); you own objective design, labs own model alignment; honest capability communication is an engineering responsibility.
**Diagrams:** (1) *graph TD* misuse vs misalignment → which defenses (yours vs labs') address each; (2) the alignment-concept → engineering-footprint table.
**Mini-project:** audit the capstone for alignment footprints: find where its objectives could be gamed (the judge, any routing reward, the agent's goal), document the reward-hacking risk of each, and add one metrics-in-tension defense (evl-01) to the most gameable. Success: a reward-hacking risk register for your own system. Capstone: the capstone's responsible-AI review; closes the module and feeds fro-05's judgment-round prep.
**Sources:** [T2] Amodei et al., Concrete Problems in AI Safety, arXiv:1606.06565 (the canonical taxonomy) · [T2] reuse Ouyang InstructGPT arXiv:2203.02155 + Sharma sycophancy arXiv:2310.13548 (fnd-07) · [T4] Anthropic Core Views on AI Safety (or current lab safety-framework post) · [T2] specification-gaming survey/DeepMind post · reuse Bai Constitutional AI arXiv:2212.08073.
**Volatile fences:** minimal — this is evergreen concepts; specific lab frameworks are the only volatile layer (light callout).
