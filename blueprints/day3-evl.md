## evl-02 — Building Eval Datasets

**Meta:** evolving/mixed · diff 3 · 180 min → 4,300–5,000 words · prereqs [evl-01] · related [evl-03, evl-05, eng-03, ftn-03]
**Thesis:** the dataset is the eval's load-bearing half — where cases come from (production, synthesis, experts), how labels stay trustworthy (agreement, adjudication), and how the set stays honest over time (held-out discipline, lifecycle, decontamination) decide whether scores mean anything.
**Sections:** (1) Intuition: a golden set is a curated sample of the job — sampling theory with editorial judgment; eng-03's case data model as the target artifact. (2) The three sources and their biases: production harvest (representative but survivor-biased toward what users tried; the flywheel mechanics — triage queue, weekly cadence); synthetic generation (scalable but distribution-shifted: LLM-generated queries are cleaner/easier than real ones, and self-preference contaminates if generator == system-under-test; mitigations: persona/difficulty conditioning, human verification sampling); expert authoring (gold quality for the hard tail and abstention cases — fnd-09's probe structure). (3) Labeling as measurement: rubric-first labeling, inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's kappa at intuition level — what 0.4 vs 0.8 means operationally), adjudication workflows, "ambiguity is a finding" (cases humans can't agree on are spec bugs — evl-01's lesson). (4) Hygiene: held-out partitions (fnd-02's leakage, prompt-layer edition), decontamination against few-shot examples AND fine-tuning corpora (ftn-03 forward link), dedup near-duplicates (fnd-06's lesson at case scale). (5) Lifecycle: owners, review-after dates, retirement of saturated cases to archive, size guidance (per-slice minimums via flip-count arithmetic — ~30+ cases per category you want to detect 10-point moves in).
**Must land:** dataset composition IS the spec (evl-01's second framing, operationalized); synthetic needs human-verification sampling forever; the flywheel is maintenance not enhancement; label disagreement is information.
**Diagrams:** (1) *graph LR* the flywheel detail: traces → triage → label → case store → suite, with the human-verification taps marked.
**Mini-project:** grow the capstone suite: harvest 15 cases from your rag-05 logs, synthesize 20 (with conditioning), author 5 hard + 5 abstention; double-label 20 with a colleague/second-judge, compute agreement, adjudicate; partition held-out. Success: a suite with provenance fields and a measured agreement number. Capstone: the eng-03 case store, populated properly.
**Sources:** [T1] Anthropic eval guide (reuse) · [T1] OpenAI evals guide (reuse) · [T2] Zheng et al. arXiv:2306.05685 (judge-vs-human agreement methodology) · [T2] Liu et al., "What Makes Good Data for Alignment?" arXiv:2312.15685 (data-quality signals) · [T5 flagged] Husain evals post (workflow detail).
**Volatile fences:** synthesis tooling only.

## evl-03 — LLM-as-Judge

**Meta:** evolving/mixed · diff 3 · 180 min → 4,300–5,000 words · prereqs [evl-01, api-03] · related [evl-02, rag-07, eng-03, fnd-07]
**Thesis:** judges make subjective quality measurable at scale, and they are reward models — every fnd-07 pathology (position/verbosity/self-preference bias, Goodharting) applies, so a judge is only as trustworthy as its calibration against humans and its blinding against gaming.
**Sections:** (1) Intuition: judging is easier than producing (fnd-07's asymmetry) — which is why judges work at all; but the judge is itself a sampled model (fnd-08) with trained preferences (fnd-07). (2) The three judge patterns: pointwise rubric scoring (the workhorse — eng-06's checklist-rubric template as the worked example), pairwise comparison (more reliable per judgment, needs position-swap to cancel position bias), reference-based (grade against a gold answer — strongest when references exist). (3) The bias catalog with mechanisms: position (first/last preference — mitigate by swap-and-average), verbosity (length ≈ quality in preference training — mitigate by length-controlled comparison), self-preference (models favor their own outputs — never judge with the generator), sycophancy toward visible desiderata (blind the judge — fnd-07's design rule). Cite the MT-Bench measurements. (4) Calibration as the license to operate: build a 50–100 item human-labeled calibration set; measure agreement (report with the evl-02 kappa vocabulary); the eng-03 rules — pinned judge config, monthly audits, falling agreement de-gates the judge. (5) Engineering the judge call: structured verdict output (api-03 — schema with per-check verdicts + quote-anchored justifications), n-run judge stability (fnd-08), small-model judges for cheap high-volume checks vs frontier judges for calibration anchoring; cost design (batch tier — api-05). (6) Goodhart watch: what happens when teams optimize against the judge (evl-01's adjective-optimizer example canonically retold) — metrics-in-tension and human audit as the standing defense.
**Must land:** unvalidated judge = opinion generator; checklist rubrics with quote anchors beat 1–10 scales (agree better, game harder); blinding is architectural not optional; the judge is part of the system config (a judge change is a baseline migration).
**Diagrams:** (1) *stateDiagram-v2* judge lifecycle: build rubric → calibrate vs humans → gate → monthly audit → (agreement falls) → recalibrate.
**Mini-project:** build the groundedness judge for your rag-05 capstone (eng-06 template): 60-item human-labeled calibration set, measure agreement, run position-swap and length-control experiments to *observe* the biases, then wire the calibrated judge into the rag-07 suite. Success: a measured agreement number and one bias personally reproduced. Capstone: eng-03's judge subsystem, live.
**Sources:** [T2] Zheng et al., MT-Bench/judge, arXiv:2306.05685 · [T2] Panickssery et al., self-preference, arXiv:2404.13076 · [T2] Verga et al., juries of smaller models, arXiv:2404.18796 · [T1] provider grader/eval docs · [T2] Sharma sycophancy arXiv:2310.13548 (reuse from fnd-07).
**Volatile fences:** which models judge well; the bias catalog and calibration doctrine are stable.

## evl-04 — Tracing & Observability

**Meta:** evolving/volatile · diff 2 · 180 min → 4,300–5,000 words · prereqs [evl-01, api-01] · related [evl-05, eng-04, eng-07]
**Thesis:** LLM systems are debugged from traces — the full record of what the model saw, produced, and cost — and the trace store is triple-duty infrastructure: debugging record, eval-case source (the flywheel's raw material), and the substrate of every eng-04 dashboard.
**Sections:** (1) Intuition: a trace is the incident's flight recorder; eng-07's first-fifteen-minutes procedure is only possible because it exists. Spans generalize: one user request = a tree (gateway → retrieval → assembly → model call(s) → tools → judges). (2) What to capture per span (the schema): full messages (post-assembly — what the model actually saw, per-region metrics from rag-01), config hash (eng-04's rule), usage/stop-reason/latency/cost (api-01), tool calls + results (eng-02's trajectory), provenance labels (sec-01 forward). (3) The emerging standard: OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions — why standardization matters (portability across the volatile platform landscape); platform category map (fence volatile: hosted LLM-observability products vs self-hosted vs OTel-native). (4) Governance: redaction before storage (PII in prompts — sec-03), retention policies, access control on traces (they contain user data + system prompts); sampling strategies (100% at low volume; head/tail sampling at scale — keep all errors + a quality sample). (5) From traces to value: the flywheel tap (evl-02), online judge sampling (evl-05 forward), cost decomposition (eng-10's audit queries), and the debugging workflow worked end-to-end on one incident.
**Must land:** log the assembled context, not the template (the #1 debugging regret); config hash on every trace; redaction is designed before the first trace is stored (eng-08's warning); traces without consumers are storage costs — wire the taps.
**Diagrams:** (1) *graph TD* a real trace tree of one RAG request with span annotations (what's recorded at each node).
**Mini-project:** instrument the capstone gateway with structured spans (JSONL or an OTel SDK): capture the schema above; then use traces alone to (a) debug one seeded failure, (b) harvest 5 eval cases, (c) produce a cost decomposition. Success: three consumers running off your own traces. Capstone: eng-04's trace store, real.
**Sources:** [T1] OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions · [T1] Langfuse docs (category exemplar) · [T1] LangSmith docs (category exemplar) · [T1] Anthropic/OpenAI usage/logging docs.
**Volatile fences:** platform landscape (heavy); the span schema and triple-duty doctrine are stable.

## evl-05 — Online Evaluation & Feedback Loops

**Meta:** evolving/mixed · diff 4 · 180 min → 4,300–5,000 words · prereqs [evl-02, evl-04] · related [evl-06, eng-04, eng-05]
**Thesis:** offline evals predict; production measures — online evaluation (feedback signals, live judge sampling, controlled experiments) closes the gap between suite scores and user reality, and feeds the flywheel that keeps offline suites honest.
**Sections:** (1) Intuition: the offline→online gap has three causes — traffic distribution shift, the suite's survivor bias, and quality dimensions users feel but cases don't encode; online eval is the corrective instrument. (2) The feedback signal hierarchy: explicit (thumbs, ratings — sparse, selection-biased toward extremes; design for it, don't trust raw rates), implicit (the richer stream: acceptance/edit-distance on suggestions, regeneration requests, copy events, abandonment, task completion, escalation-to-human) — map each to what it actually measures; instrument via evl-04's traces. (3) Live judge sampling: run calibrated judges (evl-03) on a traffic sample continuously — the quality heartbeat behind eng-04's dashboard row; drift detection before users report (the fnd-07 version-drift defense, online edition). (4) Controlled experiments for LLM features: what changes (unit of randomization — user not request, for consistency; metrics — guardrail vs goal; power reality at LLM feature scale; novelty effects), canary/shadow mechanics (eng-05 #15 at teaching depth: shadow = log-only candidate, canary = small served slice with rollback). (5) Closing the loop: online failures → triage → evl-02 cases (the flywheel's live tap); online metrics → offline suite validation (does the suite predict online movement? if not, the suite needs work — the meta-eval).
**Must land:** implicit beats explicit at volume; judge-sampled quality is a first-class SLI; experiment discipline (fnd-02's one-change rule at product scale); the suite is validated against production, not just vice versa.
**Diagrams:** (1) *graph LR* the online loop: traffic → signals/judge sample → dashboards + triage → cases + config changes → canary → traffic.
**Mini-project:** add online eval to the capstone: implicit-signal logging (regeneration, copy), a 5%-sample groundedness judge, and one shadow comparison of a prompt variant scored by the judge; report offline-suite prediction vs shadow outcome. Success: one measured offline/online agreement (or disagreement — better lesson). Capstone: the evl-05 layer of eng-04.
**Sources:** [T3] Kohavi, Tang & Xu, *Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments* (the experimentation canon) · [T2] Zheng judge arXiv:2306.05685 (sampling methodology) · [T1] OTel GenAI conventions (signal capture) · [T4] a lab engineering post on canary/shadow for ML systems (choose current at generation time).
**Volatile fences:** platform features; signal taxonomy and experiment discipline are stable.

## evl-06 — CI for LLM Applications

**Meta:** evolving/mixed · diff 3 · 180 min → 4,300–5,000 words · prereqs [evl-02, evl-03] · related [eng-04, eng-08, evl-05]
**Thesis:** the config deploy is a behavior deploy, so it gets CI — eval suites as gates, tiered for cost and latency, statistically honest about flakiness — and the same gates govern model adoptions; this chapter turns eng-04's lifecycles and eng-08's procedures into the reader's own pipeline.
**Sections:** (1) Intuition: tests gate code; evals gate behavior; the deploy pipeline for prompts/params/schemas/model-pins is CI with pass-rates instead of assertions (evl-01's generalization, now plumbed). (2) The tier architecture (eng-03's table, taught): smoke (minutes, blocking, per-PR — case selection: highest-signal representatives + recent regressions), full (nightly/pre-release, batch API — the api-05 economics that make coverage affordable), deep (model adoptions — behavioral probes + capability map). (3) Statistical gating: flakiness is physics not failure (fnd-08) — n-run pass rates with thresholds, quarantine lanes for unstable cases (with owners and expiry — never a dumping ground), flip-count-aware regression definitions (a gate that fails on noise trains the team to override gates: the eval-theater death spiral). (4) The review culture: prompt PRs carry eval diffs (sliced, not aggregate); reviewers read transcripts of new failures, not just numbers; baselines update on merge (the eng-08 procedure, step by step, as the reader's runbook). (5) Gate policies and their failure modes: hard-block vs soft-warn (hard for smoke regressions and safety cases — sec-04's red-team subset runs here; soft for judge-scored dimensions pending human audit), override protocols with audit trails, and the org-scale question: who owns a cross-team suite. (6) Cost engineering the CI itself: batch everything batchable, cache suite prefixes (api-05 — suites are the ultimate stable-prefix workload), budget per-PR eval spend.
**Must land:** gates must be trusted to be obeyed — statistical honesty is what makes them trustworthy; safety/red-team cases are hard gates from day one; model adoption uses the same pipeline at deep tier; an ungated eval is documentation cosplay (evl-01's line — reuse it).
**Diagrams:** (1) *graph LR* the gated pipeline: PR → smoke → full → canary → rollout, with gate policies annotated per stage (the eng-04 lifecycle diagram, expanded with policy detail).
**Mini-project:** wire the capstone suite into actual CI (GitHub Actions or equivalent): smoke tier on PR (n=2, hard gate), full tier nightly on batch, a quarantine lane, and one deliberate regression to watch the gate catch it; document the override protocol. Success: a PR blocked by a behavior regression you introduced on purpose. Capstone: the capstone becomes self-protecting; the curriculum's artifact chain closes its loop.
**Sources:** [T1] Anthropic eval guide (reuse) · [T1] OpenAI evals + batch docs (reuse) · [T5 flagged] Husain evals post (CI workflow detail) · [T3] Kohavi et al. (guardrail-metric concept, reuse from evl-05).
**Volatile fences:** CI platform specifics only.
